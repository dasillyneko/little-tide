# A 2-minute-40-second demonstration

Record a short real Twitch broadcast. Capture the broadcast/player, Twitch chat, the control room and this Codex build conversation. Keep subscription rewards at 0 for the recording. No background music is necessary.

Before recording: use Just the ocean; set Feed cooldown to 3 seconds, Feeds per arrival to 1, Entry window to 15 seconds, and leave announcements off. These are genuine streamer controls, not simulated events. You can restore your preferred values afterward. Do not wait for a random appearance; use Invite a moonwhale and explain that the streamer can initiate it.

## 0:00–0:15 — The experience

Show the aquarium in OBS/the live Twitch broadcast.

Say: “This is Little Tide, a living ASCII ocean for Twitch communities. It gives viewers a calm, shared activity during stream breaks and Starting Soon screens.”

## 0:15–0:50 — A real viewer action

Show the actual Twitch chat and send `!feed`. Show the food, ripples, animals responding and the new arrival. Mention that arrival frequency is adjustable.

Say: “This command comes from real Twitch chat through EventSub. Food lands in the ocean, animals swim toward it, and community feeding welcomes new friends. The server uses Twitch user IDs for cooldowns and ignores duplicate messages.”

## 0:50–1:20 — A rare encounter

Click Invite a moonwhale, show its silver body, and send `!catch` in Twitch chat. Wait through the 15-second window. Open Discoveries and show the result.

Say: “A rare moonwhale can appear after feeding, or the streamer can invite one. Every viewer gets one free catch entry. When the window closes, the server selects an explorer and saves their discovery.”

## 1:20–1:55 — Make it your stream

Open Design. Switch light/dark once. Change to Starting Soon, edit the title, and click Apply text to overlay. Show the updated OBS/broadcast. Keep these changes short and rehearsed.

Say: “The streamer controls the appearance. Scene text, fonts, colors, positioning and effects are editable. Changes are saved per channel and reach the OBS view through the same local service.”

## 1:55–2:25 — Built with Codex

Show this actual build conversation with a user instruction and the resulting code changes. Briefly show the test result or relevant Twitch code.

Say: “I directed the idea and iterations with OpenAI Codex. Codex helped implement the Twitch authorization and EventSub integration, gameplay rules, design editor, rendering changes and tests. The app keeps OAuth tokens on the local server and reconnects when Twitch interrupts a session.”

## 2:25–2:40 — Credit and close

Return to the aquarium.

Say: “The rendering foundation comes from the MIT-licensed ASCII Sharks project. Our work adds the aquatic creatures, feeding, Twitch integration, encounters, persistence and streamer tools. Little Tide turns a quiet stream screen into something the community builds together.”

## After recording

Check that the video is under 3 minutes, audible and readable. Make sure real chat actions and the Codex build footage are included. Upload to YouTube as Public, then test the link while signed out. Do not show the contents of data/twitch.json or credential screens.
